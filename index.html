Daniel Duclos-Cavalcanti

overview

I'm an electrical and computer engineer living in New York with a B.Sc. and M.Sc. from the Technical University of Munich. Today, I work at Ripple as a software engineer building custody infrastructure in Rust.

I was born in Minneapolis to Brazilian parents that emigrated to pursue their PhDs in Economics. I moved shortly after to Brazil, and eventually pursued higher education in Germany. It does dawn on me that having found a natural pull towards Blockchain and fintech infrastructure, as an engineer son to economists, does feel almost obvious, but to me it was a happy happenstance.

I've always been curious towards machines, how programs are consumed, decoded into instructions and then executed as specified. That pulled me across many layers of computing abstraction: digital electronics, computer architecture, FPGAs, operating systems, Linux, and networking. Finally, the software-engineering that ties these structures together and the capability to drive them in a distributed fashion towards a single end-purpose was always fascinating to me.

My interests lie around high-performance distributed computing, blockchain, and system-level engineering. Feel free to reach out via the contact information on this page.

industry

At Ripple, I began on the Key Management System (KMS): a unified abstraction over heterogeneous HSM and MPC backends for secure key storage, wrapping, and cryptographic signing. More recently I've been building Managed Custody, a greenfield Rust product that orchestrates Ripple Custody over AWS infrastructure for tier-1 and tier-2 banks.

research

I have collaborated in the past on independent research with Dr. Anirudh Sivaraman and Dr. Muhammad Haseeb of Systems@NYU on distributed, low-latency networking for financial exchanges in the cloud. The work centered around Onyx, a highly scalable and performant financial exchange in the cloud, published at ACM SIGCOMM '25, a flagship venue in computer networking. My master's thesis built Viveiro, a heuristic that selects VMs by present cloud conditions to improve a multicast tree's latency, both before and at runtime, which informed Onyx's design.
